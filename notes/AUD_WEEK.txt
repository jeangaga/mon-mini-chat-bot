# A. AUD WEEK — MACRO SYNTHESIS

Australia moved into a **near-term inflation-relief / resilient-demand regime**, with the RBA's previously hawkish near-term bias materially weakened by the data flow. Q2 CPI delivered the first broad downside surprise in the recent sequence: headline, trimmed mean and weighted median all undershot consensus, while the policy-critical annual trimmed mean **held at 3.6% rather than accelerating as the RBA had expected**. That print arrived only one day after Governor Bullock had reiterated a forceful pre-emptive tightening doctrine, creating a genuine communication/state transition: the trigger for an August hike failed to materialise and the market rapidly converged on an extended hold.
But the macro tape does not support a dovish-cycle call. Import prices surged 5.7% q/q, PPI accelerated to 1.3% q/q, credit remained firm, residential approvals rebounded sharply and July manufacturing/services PMIs showed a broader domestic activity re-acceleration. Housing prices simultaneously weakened materially, adding evidence that 4.35% is already restrictive. The resulting regime is therefore **underlying inflation stalled rather than defeated + resilient activity/credit + restrictive housing transmission + adverse upstream cost risk**. The near-term policy state shifts decisively to **extended hold with hawkish language retained**, while the tightening tail now requires fresh evidence that upstream costs are passing into trimmed-mean/services inflation rather than simply remaining elevated.

2. Professional Market Commentary

The professional framing was a broad downside inflation surprise that removed the immediate case for another RBA hike, rather than simple fuel-driven headline relief.

Q2 headline CPI rose 0.6% q/q versus 0.7% expected, while both main quarterly underlying measures also undershot: trimmed mean at 0.8% versus 0.9% and weighted median at 0.7% versus 0.8%. Annual trimmed mean and weighted median were both 3.6% against 3.7% expectations.

Release-day market pricing largely stripped the probability of an August hike, while AUD and Australian government yields fell.

The composition nevertheless prevented a clean “inflation solved” interpretation. Housing remained the major annual contributor, while rents, insurance, healthcare and other domestically oriented services remained sticky. Fuel provided an unusually large disinflationary contribution as world oil prices eased during June and Australian fuel-excise relief remained in effect.

The institutional split therefore shifted from “August hike versus hold” toward “extended hold versus pause within a still-live tightening bias.”

3. Comment — Economist Layer

This is the first inflation release in the recent Australian sequence where the policy-relevant quarterly core measures join the headline on the downside of consensus.

Signal hierarchy:

* Quarterly headline CPI: 0.6% q/q versus 0.7% expected.
* Quarterly trimmed mean: 0.8% versus 0.9% expected.
* Quarterly weighted median: 0.7% versus 0.8% expected.
* June headline CPI: -0.1% m/m and 3.8% y/y, both softer than consensus.
* Monthly trimmed mean: 0.3% m/m / 3.6% y/y.
* Domestic services and housing inflation remain substantially firmer than the aggregate headline.

The headline disinflation is partly lower-quality. Automotive fuel fell sharply during June after already declining in May, reflecting both lower world oil prices and fuel-excise relief. Housing inflation remained elevated, including electricity, new dwellings and rents.

The correct decomposition is therefore:

tradables / energy disinflation
versus
sticky domestic services / housing inflation.

But the quarterly underlying miss means the release cannot be dismissed as an energy-only event. The RBA's prior inflation path had anticipated materially more Q2 headline inflation and somewhat stronger underlying pressure.

The burden of proof consequently changes. Before this print, the RBA needed evidence that earlier tightening was working sufficiently to justify waiting. After it, a near-term hike requires renewed evidence of persistence from subsequent inflation, labour and domestic-demand data.

State transition: reversal.

Context from prior regional live file:
Australia's preceding CPI sequence had repeatedly delivered headline relief without enough underlying relief, with the earlier tape retaining tightening optionality because trimmed mean, weighted median and non-tradables inflation remained firm. Q2 CPI breaks that pattern because both quarterly core measures now undershoot consensus alongside the headline. The inflation constraint remains above target, but the trajectory moves from renewed tightening pressure toward a credible pause regime.

4. HF Take — Strategist Layer

The release decisively lowers the hurdle for an August hold but does not establish an easing cycle.

Interpretation:

* immediate hike pressure falls sharply;
* disinflation credibility improves because core measures also miss;
* domestic services inflation remains too high for a clean dovish pivot;
* the June fuel decline carries an important vintage risk because subsequent oil normalization can reverse part of the headline relief.

AU front-end dovish repricing is justified, but a later-2026 tightening branch remains live if July inflation reaccelerates or domestic activity stays strong.

For AUD, the immediate signal is negative through relative rates. The medium-term FX implication depends on whether softer realised inflation survives stronger activity and the upstream cost shock visible in subsequent releases.

Next verification tests: July monthly CPI, labour-market cooling, household demand, and whether services/non-tradables inflation follows the headline lower.

THURSDAY 30 JUL 2026

Australia — International Trade Price Indexes (Q2)

Release Date: 30 Jul 2026 | Local Time: 03:30
Importance: ***

1. Reuters Data

Export Prices: 1,1% | Prior: 0,5%
Import Prices: 5,7% | Prior: 0,1%

2. Professional Market Commentary

This is a large external cost-shock print rather than a conventional demand-inflation release.

Import prices rose 5.7% q/q, the largest quarterly increase since late 2021. Petroleum and related-product import prices surged by roughly 47% in the quarter, with large increases also recorded in fertilisers and primary-form plastics.

The ABS explicitly associated the move with supply disruption linked to the closure of the Strait of Hormuz.

Export prices increased only 1.1% q/q. Coal, petroleum products and other commodity exports benefited from the global energy shock, but aggregate export-price growth lagged the import-price surge by a wide margin.

The publication therefore provides unusually direct official evidence that the Middle East supply shock had moved from financial-market pricing into measured Australian import costs.

3. Comment — Economist Layer

The important feature is the abrupt reversal in the import-price trajectory.

Q1 had shown:

* Export Prices: +0.5% q/q
* Import Prices: +0.1% q/q

That had suggested the external oil shock had not yet translated into broad aggregate import inflation.

Q2 changes the picture materially:

* import prices accelerate from +0.1% to +5.7% q/q;
* petroleum prices register an extreme quarterly increase;
* fertiliser and plastics prices also rise sharply;
* the official release directly identifies supply disruption as the mechanism.

The export side provides only a partial offset. Coal and petroleum export prices benefit from tighter world energy markets, but the aggregate export-price gain remains much smaller than the import-price shock.

The price terms-of-trade impulse is therefore sharply adverse at the quarterly margin. This should not be mechanically equated with the national-accounts terms of trade, but directionally it represents a materially worse external-price configuration than Q1.

For inflation, the sequencing matters more than the immediate consumer-price effect:

Hormuz shock
→ imported petroleum / material prices
→ domestic producer and freight costs
→ potential CPI pass-through.

This is therefore an important counterweight to the previous day's softer CPI. CPI says realised Q2 consumer inflation was weaker than feared; trade prices say the upstream cost environment entering Q3 is substantially less benign.

State transition: reversal — external cost shock.

Context from prior regional live file:
Q1 trade prices had shown virtually flat import inflation and left Australia's inflation story dominated by domestic CPI and credit. Q2 overturns that benign upstream configuration as import prices jump 5.7%. The external-price channel moves from secondary relief to a meaningful prospective inflation risk.

4. HF Take — Strategist Layer

This does not reverse the near-term RBA message from Q2 CPI because import-price shocks pass through with lags and are not equivalent to persistent domestic demand inflation.

It does weaken the case for extrapolating the CPI miss into sustained disinflation.

RBA relevance is conditional:

* if imported energy and material costs remain concentrated in volatile categories, the Board can look through much of the move;
* if those costs spread into freight, construction, services and trimmed-mean CPI, later-2026 tightening risk rises materially;
* Australia's external-price mix is also less straightforwardly AUD-positive than a normal commodity shock because imports are repricing faster than aggregate exports.

The verification test is domestic pass-through. The following day's PPI provides the first answer.

1. Q2 CPI — realised consumer inflation softer than expected.
2. Q2 import prices — major upstream cost shock.

Reaction-function split:

That argues for hold with optionality rather than either renewed aggressive tightening or a dovish pivot.

For AUD rates, the CPI/PPI combination favours less immediate hike premium while preserving later-2026 inflation risk.

# D. CENTRAL BANK TAPE

## Summary — Policy reaction function

The window contains a sharp **communication-to-data reversal**. Governor Michele Bullock's 28 July Anika Foundation address maintained the RBA's explicit tightening bias and reinforced the doctrine that delaying necessary restraint could ultimately require higher rates and greater employment costs. The 29 July CPI then failed the Board's own key test: underlying inflation did **not** rise as expected. Annual trimmed mean remained at 3.6%, headline inflation eased to 3.8%, and the immediate August hike case was rapidly stripped from market and major-bank forecasts.

### Bottom line

The relevant pair is **AUD/USD**. That rates shock did **not** generate a comparable bearish FX-consensus turn. By 31 July AUD/USD had recovered to **0.7022**. MUFG's 3 August forecast was comparatively cautious at **0.7000 for Q3 and 0.7100 for Q4**, but ING's 6 August update was explicitly **mildly bullish**, with spot at 0.704 and targets of **0.71 at 1M, 0.73 at 3M/6M and 0.74 at 12M**. The window therefore contains an important distinction between **RBA forecast repricing and AUD forecast repricing**. The former became materially more dovish after CPI; the latter remained constructive and, in ING's case, its explicit forecast path was raised between the July and 6 August publications. AUD/USD recovered above 0.70 before the window closed even though the immediate RBA hike probability had effectively disappeared.

**Public-bank short-horizon / Q3 range:** approximately **0.70–0.73** in the available comparable public sample.

**Constructive AUD cluster:** approximately **0.71–0.73**.

### What changed through the data window

The start of the window still contained a meaningful RBA-hike premium. That support was abruptly reduced by the 29 July CPI release: core inflation undershot both consensus and the RBA forecast, AUD fell to roughly 0.695 and the probability of an August increase was almost completely removed.

The subsequent recovery in AUD therefore occurred **despite**, rather than because of, a renewed near-term RBA tightening story. By 31 July MUFG's spot reference was 0.7022, and ING noted on 4 August that AUD had initially underperformed NZD after the CPI-driven dovish repricing.

By 6 August ING had upgraded to a mildly bullish 0.71 one-month / 0.73 three-month profile. Its reasoning was that the market had already stripped most additional RBA tightening, reducing the downside from further front-end repricing, while Australian carry, terms of trade and broader fundamentals remained supportive.

The point-in-time transition was consequently **from RBA-supported AUD to a more diversified bullish case**: less incremental support from Australian rate expectations, but stronger reliance on carry already embedded in the level of rates, relative domestic resilience, commodity/terms-of-trade support and the prospect that Fed tightening expectations would eventually soften.

### Main consensus drivers

**RBA differential / carry:**
The CPI miss removed the immediate August-hike story, but Australia still retained one of the more attractive G10 carry profiles. With only around 12bp of tightening left in year-end pricing by 6 August, further dovish RBA repricing posed less downside to AUD than before.

**Fed / broad USD regime:**
The constructive AUD case depended materially on eventual softer US rate pricing. The more constructive public forecasts linked AUD upside to reduced Fed hawkishness, while the more cautious view remained concerned that the dollar could stay supported against high-beta G10 currencies if global rates/risk conditions deteriorated.

**Terms of trade / commodities:**
Australia's underlying commodity/terms-of-trade position remained an important differentiator versus other commodity currencies, even if softer energy prices reduced part of the immediate windfall. This supported a constructive medium-term AUD view independent of another RBA hike.

**Global risk beta:**
AUD remained exposed to the broader risk regime. Persistently high global yields and renewed financial-market volatility could weigh particularly on high-beta G10 currencies. Thus the same Middle East shock that could preserve RBA tightening optionality could become AUD-negative if its dominant transmission shifted from commodities/rates toward global risk aversion.

### Where consensus could be wrong

**Consensus too bullish AUD:**
If the Q2 CPI downside proves persistent, the RBA tightening tail disappears entirely, while the Fed remains relatively hawkish and higher global yields generate a risk-off episode, the attractive-carry/commodity narrative may be insufficient to sustain AUD/USD above 0.70. MUFG's 0.7000 Q3 target captures this more cautious near-term configuration.

**Consensus too cautious AUD:**
If the stronger credit/PMI/activity evidence persists and the import-price/PPI shock begins to pass through into underlying inflation, the RBA may retain or rebuild a genuine later-2026 tightening premium. Combined with eventual dovish Fed repricing, that would make the 0.71–0.73 target cluster more plausible and could leave the 0.70 cautious target too low.

**Key falsification:**
The critical combination is **Australian core inflation/pass-through versus US rate repricing**. Sustained softer Australian core inflation together with a still-hawkish Fed would falsify the constructive AUD view; renewed domestic inflation persistence while Fed hike expectations fade would materially strengthen it.
