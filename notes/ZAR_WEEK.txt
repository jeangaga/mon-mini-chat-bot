A. ZAR WEEK — MACRO SYNTHESIS

South Africa ended the period in a **weak-demand / easing-cost / resilient-external-buffer** configuration. Forward activity deteriorated as the leading indicator largely unwound its March acceleration, private-sector credit slowed materially and PMI new orders contracted for a third month, while hiring momentum softened. Against that weak domestic backdrop, the inflation impulse improved upstream: June PPI reversed the May sequential shock and July PMI input-cost pressure eased as fuel prices fell. The external side remained the clear offset, with a large June trade-surplus beat and no reserve-stress signal.

The policy read shifted mildly dovish at the margin without becoming an easing regime. Kganyago defended the 23 July 4–2 hold, reaffirmed the 3% inflation anchor and pointed back to a pre-shock model path toward roughly 6%, while refusing fixed forward guidance. In-window oil easing and rand recovery made the hold increasingly comfortable, but 5.0% CPI, elevated services inflation, higher expectations and the still-live inflation threshold keep a September hike tail open. The regime is therefore a **conditional hawkish hold with mild dovish consolidation**: weak demand and post-shock disinflation favour patience, while downstream inflation persistence remains the binding constraint.

Growth: -
Supporting evidence:
The leading indicator fell again in May and has almost fully unwound the March acceleration; PSCE slowed to 7.80% from 8.57%; and July PMI new orders contracted for a third month despite output returning to only marginal growth. Labor: -
Supporting evidence:

Inflation: ~
Supporting evidence:
June PPI cooled to 7.5% y/y with a -0.1% m/m print, reversing May’s acute sequential surge, while July PMI showed falling fuel prices easing input-cost inflation and second-round wage effects. However, the policy anchor still carried 5.0% CPI, elevated services inflation, firmer underlying measures and higher inflation expectations, leaving downstream persistence unresolved.

Financial Conditions: ~
Supporting evidence:
Private-sector credit cooled materially and the yield-curve component was a drag on the leading indicator, consistent with restrictive conditions. Offsetting that, M3 growth remained firm at 9.31%, net reserves improved in July, no reserve-defence regime was evident, and the June trade surplus rebuilt the external cushion.

Policy Constraint: ~
Supporting evidence:
SARB held at 7.00% by 4–2 after the prior hike, with the split reversing from May and Kganyago arguing that the earlier tightening had created room to wait. The central-bank tape reinforces a conditional hold bias: oil eased, the rand recovered and the July forecast cut 2026 CPI to 4.0% while lifting GDP to 1.4%. Kganyago also re-anchored the eventual pre-shock policy path near 6%. However, two July dissenters preferred another hike, only one additional vote is required to flip the committee, and persistent inflation remains the key September hawkish trigger. The constraint is therefore unresolved rather than dovish: patience is increasingly justified, but inflation persistence can still force renewed tightening.
