## 1. MARKET / REGIME

### MARKET STATE

The cleanest description is:

**Concentrated earnings-led resilience at index level, with a long-end rates shock driving deterioration beneath the surface.**

This is not broad risk-off:

- SPX only **−0.27% w/w** to 7,722.72;
- Nasdaq remained positive on the week and made a new intraday high Friday;
- NVDA made its first record since May;
- VIX finished only **15.31**.

But it is not healthy broadening either:

- 10Y **+7bp w/w**, peak 5.34%;
- 30Y **+11bp**;
- 2Y **−5bp**;
- Dow/SPX negative versus Nasdaq positive;
- only **44.1%** of SPX above its 200dma versus 47.8% a week earlier on the supplied breadth series;
- second consecutive week of US equity outflows, **−$2.7bn**;
- strongest long-duration bond inflow since May 2025.

The yield curve move matters. This was **bear steepening at the long end while front-end Fed expectations softened**. Therefore the equity constraint is no longer adequately described as simply “Fed hawkishness.”

### WHAT CHANGED

**Strengthened:** concentration / AI leadership.

**Strengthened:** sensitivity of non-leader equities to long-end yields.

**Weakened:** case for near-term Fed tightening.

**Not yet changed:** aggregate earnings support.

**Still unresolved:** whether weak breadth is a temporary rates dislocation or a more structural narrowing.

VIX at 15.31 versus 30-day realized volatility around 10.5 suggests investors are paying some premium for protection, but this is still far from a panic-volatility regime.

## 2. WHAT DROVE THE MARKET

### #1 — GLOBAL LONG-END SELLOFF

This was the dominant weekly driver.

The move was global:

- US 10Y reached **5.34%**, highest since 2002;
- UK 30Y traded above **6%**;
- Japanese 10Y above **3.1%**;
- OAT-Bund around **140bp**, highest since the 2011–12 period.

The equity transmission was clearest Thursday: Treasuries caught a safe-haven bid as French stress intensified, yields fell, and US equities reversed their intraday losses.

The crucial distinction is:

**front-end Fed expectations softened while long-end yields rose.**

This points toward term premium, fiscal/supply concerns, inflation uncertainty and capital-demand pressure rather than only the expected Fed path.

Cross-sectionally, that pressure was absorbed poorly by the broad market but surprisingly well by AI/semiconductor leaders.

---

### #2 — OCTOBER FED-HIKE TAIL WAS REMOVED

Williams Tuesday: **“no need for urgency.”**

Evercore ISI interpreted that as a clear rejection of another consecutive October hike.

Friday payrolls then printed:

- **+29k vs +90k expected** in the scout baseline;
- August revised to +133k;
- unemployment 4.2%;
- hourly earnings +0.1% m/m.

Estimated October hike probability fell from roughly **47% Tuesday to 20% Friday**.

The initial market move was textbook:

**weak employment → lower Fed risk → yields down → equities up.**

But the persistence was not textbook.

The 10Y reversed from approximately **5.18% to 5.28%**, while equities retained their gains.

That tells us Friday's equity rally was primarily about **removing near-term policy-tail risk**, not a belief that the long-end problem had disappeared.

---

### #3 — IRAN / OIL FED THE RATES CHANNEL

Trump's rejection of Iranian conditions contributed to the initial rates/inflation move early in the week.

Friday's G7 announcement of a **100Mb crude/diesel release** pushed in the opposite direction.

For equities, oil was primarily a **rates/inflation transmission variable**, not the dominant direct sector story.

## 3. REACTION FUNCTION / SURPRISES

### MATTERED

**Long-end yields:** consistently mattered to broad equity performance.

**Williams / NFP:** materially changed near-term Fed pricing.

**Treasury relief Thursday:** directly helped turn US equities higher intraday.

---

### IGNORED

**Core PCE 3.0% y/y vs 3.3% expected.**

Despite a benign inflation surprise, SPX finished Wednesday lower and yields remained resilient.

Nathan Peterson/Schwab explicitly highlighted the surprise that rates remained so firm despite softer PCE, weaker employment evidence and reduced Fed-hike expectations.

**Consumer confidence 81.9**, lowest since 2014, produced no identifiable index reaction.

**Anthropic leaked prospectus:** approximately $518bn of cloud/compute commitments and a 2025 net loss of $41.97bn did not derail the AI complex.

---

### COUNTER-INTUITIVE

**1. Softer inflation no longer guarantees lower long-end yields.**

This raises the importance of term premium, fiscal supply and structural capital demand.

**2. Post-NFP yields rebounded without killing the equity rally.**

Equities differentiated between:

- lower near-term Fed risk;
- still-high long-end discount rates.

**3. “Bad news = good news” survived another week.**

Todd Schoenberger/CrossCheck framed weak payrolls as constructive because bonds had already tightened financial conditions.

But Justin Wolfers' observation that three-month payroll growth is only around **+17k/month** identifies the boundary: further weakness can eventually become an earnings/growth problem rather than a Fed-relief catalyst.

### CURRENT REACTION FUNCTION

**Weak macro = equity-positive while it lowers Fed risk without creating an earnings scare.**

**Higher long-end yields = broad-market negative, but AI/semi leaders remain partially insulated while their earnings/revision momentum stays exceptional.**

The key transition to watch is therefore:

**when does higher long-end yield finally overwhelm leader earnings resilience?**

## 6. EARNINGS / REVISIONS

### REVISION TREND

Near-term aggregate earnings evidence remains strong.

FactSet tape supplied:

- Q3 EPS growth expectation **+29.5%**;
- versus **+26.7%** at 30 June;
- forward P/E approximately **19.0x**.

This matters because weak breadth is **not yet being confirmed by aggregate earnings deterioration**.

The market currently has:

**better earnings revisions + worse participation.**

That divergence is central.

---

### GUIDANCE

Approximately **62%** of companies issuing guidance in the supplied FactSet sample have given positive guidance.

Near-term corporate fundamentals therefore remain support rather than the source of weakness.

---

### REACTION ASYMMETRY

This is where the evidence became more interesting this week.

**MU:** huge beat-and-raise, muted reaction versus implied.

**ACN:** strong guidance triggered a violent rally, but a significant portion was immediately retraced.

The market is therefore starting to distinguish between:

- fundamental strength;
- already-high expectations;
- positioning;
- durability of scarcity economics.

---

### WHAT IS NOW PRICED

The question into Q3 earnings is no longer:

**Can earnings beat?**

Aggregate expectations already assume very strong earnings.

The more important question is:

**Can strong earnings still generate positive stock reactions?**

Walter Todd/Greenwood highlights the next comparison problem: LSEG consensus implies roughly **+15% EPS growth in 2027 after +35% in 2026**.

That is not current deterioration.

It is the forward hurdle.

## 9. PM CARRY / NEXT WEEK

### BASE CASE

SPX/NDX can remain resilient near highs while underlying participation remains poor because **AI/semiconductor earnings leadership is still strong enough to absorb the long-end shock**.

The broad market remains more fragile than the index.

---

### CONFIRMATION

The current view strengthens if:

- 10Y retests 5.30–5.34% and NDX/SOX/NVDA still hold leadership;
- earnings revisions remain positive;
- bad macro continues to reduce Fed risk without causing equity weakness;
- breadth stops deteriorating even without a large yield rally.

---

### WARNING

The first important warning is not another weak breadth statistic.

It is:

**strong company news producing increasingly weak reactions in the leaders.**

MU is the first yellow flag.

A sequence of similar reactions would suggest expectations have become the binding constraint.

---

### REGIME BREAK

**Negative break:**

10Y moves sustainably above **5.34%** and AI/semiconductor leaders break relative performance.

That would mean the rates shock has finally reached the group supporting the index.

A second negative break would be:

**weak macro → lower yields → lower equities**, indicating “bad news = good news” has turned into a growth scare.

**Positive regime change:**

Financials / Industrials / Small Caps begin outperforming **without requiring materially lower yields**.

That would be credible broadening rather than simple duration relief.

---

### MAIN ASYMMETRY

The highest-information outcome is:

**good fundamental news with weak stock reactions.**

With Q3 earnings expectations already exceptionally strong and strategist targets clustered only modestly above spot, upside now requires more than simply beating consensus.
