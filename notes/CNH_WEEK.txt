# A. CNH WEEK — MACRO SYNTHESIS

China ended the window in a **K-shaped external-strength / weak-private-demand regime**, with the credit data providing materially stronger evidence of domestic weakness than the inflation prints alone. July CPI slowed to 0.5% y/y and remained negative m/m, while PPI decelerated to 3.5% y/y with a second consecutive monthly decline. The inflation signal is not generalized deflation: core CPI remained +0.9%, services +0.7% and a large part of the CPI downside reflected food and transport-energy mechanics. But weak goods pricing and fading producer-price momentum remain consistent with deficient final demand.

The JGM overlay sharpens the composition of growth. China’s external sector remains supported by the global AI-capex and green-tech cycles, but the strongest export categories are increasingly capital-intensive, while lower-tech labour-intensive sectors such as textiles, footwear and toys are stagnating. Strong headline exports can therefore coexist with persistent pressure on employment/income transmission and weak household demand: **external resilience is not necessarily translating into broad domestic income creation**. This is consistent with the Politburo’s balanced-development objective and makes the economy increasingly K-shaped.

The decisive domestic deterioration came through balance sheets. M2 slowed to 7.7% and July new yuan lending contracted by a record CNY340bn, with both household and corporate borrowing negative despite declining borrowing costs. JGM reads weak household lending—especially mortgages—as another reflection of the still-soft property market, while weak medium/long-term corporate borrowing points to subdued capex demand. Government-bond issuance kept broader TSF growth stable, producing a clear **fiscal substitution** configuration: public financing is sustaining aggregate liquidity while private-sector willingness to lever remains weak.

The validated PBoC tape sharpens the policy interpretation. Both previously identified easing triggers effectively fired during the window—**PPI peaked and private credit demand collapsed**—yet the PBoC deliberately withheld a conventional easing signal, reiterating an “appropriately loose” stance and “measures as needed” while PBoC-backed commentary pushed investors toward a broader financing lens rather than the bank-loan contraction alone. The regime is therefore **fiscal-led easing under intensifying dovish data pressure: room to ease restored, need for support rising, but institutional response still withheld**. JGM additionally sees accelerated implementation of existing domestic-demand measures—particularly childcare, elderly care and experience-led services—as potentially supportive of services prices, but only if consumer confidence improves alongside the labour and property cycles.

2. Professional Market Commentary

The professional tape reads July as a materially softer headline-inflation print, but not yet as a clean return to economy-wide deflation.

CPI slowed to 0.5% y/y from 1.0%, while the monthly index fell 0.1% against expectations for +0.2%. The official NBS breakdown shows food prices down 1.5% y/y, non-food inflation at +0.9%, consumer goods at +0.2% and services at +0.7%. Core CPI excluding food and energy remained positive at +0.9% y/y and +0.3% m/m. ([Statistiques nationales](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202608/t20260810_1965018.html "Consumer Price Index in July 2026"))

The institutional debate is therefore between two interpretations. The more bearish framing sees the triple inflation miss as domestic-demand weakness re-emerging beneath the earlier commodity shock. The more cautious interpretation stresses that the headline deceleration was heavily driven by reversible energy mechanics while services and core inflation remained positive. The scout records Pinpoint, ANZ, ING and Nomura variants of this debate.

No clean release-specific market reaction was identified.

3. Comment — Economist Layer

The headline is clearly softer than expected, but the composition matters.

Key signals:

* CPI y/y fell from 1.0% to 0.5%, missing consensus by 0.3pp.
* CPI m/m remained negative at -0.1%, versus +0.2% expected.
* Food prices fell 1.5% y/y, with livestock meat -6.0% and pork -13.3%; pork alone remains a material mechanical drag on the aggregate. ([Statistiques nationales](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202608/t20260810_1965018.html "Consumer Price Index in July 2026"))
* Consumer goods inflation was only +0.2% y/y, consistent with weak pricing power in the goods economy.
* Transportation and telecommunication prices fell 2.2% m/m; energy for transport facilities fell 9.8% m/m. This is the clearest mechanical source of the monthly downside surprise. ([Statistiques nationales](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202608/t20260810_1965018.html "Consumer Price Index in July 2026"))
* The counter-signal is the sticky domestic-service leg: services rose 0.4% m/m and 0.7% y/y.
* Core CPI excluding food and energy rose 0.3% m/m and 0.9% y/y.
* Travel-agency/touring services rose 6.6% m/m and medical services 4.3% y/y, demonstrating that the entire domestic basket is not deflationary. ([Statistiques nationales](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202608/t20260810_1965018.html "Consumer Price Index in July 2026"))
* The 2026 CPI base rotation is estimated by NBS to affect monthly y/y CPI by roughly 0.06pp on average, worth retaining as a small measurement caveat. ([Statistiques nationales](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202608/t20260810_1965018.html "Consumer Price Index in July 2026"))

The hierarchy is therefore:

headline disinflation > energy unwind + food drag > weak goods pricing > still-positive core/services.

July does strengthen the low-inflation / deficient-demand story, but the 0.5% headline should not be treated as a pure domestic-demand signal because a large part of the sequential move comes from the transport-energy reversal.

The positive core/services CPI readings therefore matter: stronger implementation of consumption-support measures could stabilize that part of the basket even as earlier energy-driven inflation loses momentum.

State transition: deterioration — headline inflation loses momentum sharply and reopens the deflation-risk debate, but core and services prevent a full return to a generalized deflation regime.

The professional implication is that China's earlier commodity-driven producer-price acceleration is losing momentum without having generated broad downstream pricing power.

3. Comment — Economist Layer

The level-versus-momentum distinction is the core of this release.

Key signals:

* PPI remained high at +3.5% y/y, but slowed from 4.1% and undershot consensus by 0.3pp.
* The scout records PPI falling 0.7% m/m, a second consecutive monthly decline.
* Upstream mining and commodity categories continue to support the annual rate.
* Downstream categories such as food manufacturing, beverages and pharmaceuticals remained much weaker in the institutional decomposition.
* Weather and construction disruptions contributed to some monthly weakness, so the sequential decline is not entirely a clean demand signal.
* Nevertheless, a second negative monthly print alongside weak CPI goods pricing suggests the upstream price shock is not transmitting strongly through domestic final demand.

The important macro message is not that China has returned to producer-price deflation—the y/y rate is still +3.5%. It is that **the producer-price impulse is fading at the margin while downstream pricing power remains weak**.

State transition: deterioration — upstream inflation remains elevated in level terms, but sequential momentum has turned negative and the pass-through into consumer-facing prices remains poor.

Context from prior regional live file:
The prior China tape already distinguished commodity/input-price pressure from weak domestic final demand. July preserves that split: PPI remains elevated because of upstream sectors, but falling monthly prices and weak consumer-price transmission argue against interpreting the annual rate as evidence of strong nominal domestic activity.

4. HF Take — Strategist Layer

PPI is less directly policy-relevant than CPI or credit, but it strengthens the week's broader nominal-demand diagnosis.

Reaction-function map:

* declining monthly producer prices reduce the risk that earlier commodity inflation becomes a persistent domestic inflation shock;
* weak downstream pass-through gives the PBOC more inflation space to remain accommodative;
* however, the positive 3.5% annual PPI rate means policymakers are unlikely to frame the situation as outright industrial deflation;
* the more important policy question becomes whether softer input-price momentum translates into better real household purchasing power or simply reflects weaker industrial demand.

For CNH, the signal is mildly negative at the margin because the data combination points to weak domestic demand rather than benign disinflation generated by stronger productivity or supply.

Next verification test: industrial profits, PMI output prices and August PPI momentum.

# E. SIGNAL TENSION CHECK

Main contradiction:
China’s headline external performance remains supported by **AI/green-tech and other capital-intensive export sectors**, while the domestic economy is showing record private-credit weakness, weak mortgages/capex borrowing, soft inflation and stagnation in lower-tech labour-intensive exporters. Both PBoC easing triggers have fired, yet the Bank has withheld a conventional cut signal.

Why it matters:
Strong export growth can materially overstate the health of the household/labour cycle when the winning sectors are capital-intensive. At the same time, deteriorating credit demand strengthens the case for support while demonstrating why another small rate cut may have limited transmission.

Implication:
The policy problem is increasingly **distribution and transmission rather than system liquidity**. Fiscal/targeted support needs to broaden growth toward households, services, property-linked confidence and labour-intensive activity; otherwise strong external headlines can coexist with persistent domestic lowflation and weak private leverage.
