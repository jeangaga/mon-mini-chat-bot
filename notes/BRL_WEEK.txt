DATA WINDOW: 23 Jul 2026 to 7 Aug 2026

A. BRL WEEK — MACRO SYNTHESIS

Brazil exits the 23 Jul–7 Aug window in a cleaner cyclical disinflation/slowdown regime: IPCA-15, FGV/Fipe inflation gauges and PMI price indices softened together, while hard industrial production rolled over and both manufacturing and services PMIs moved into contraction. The external account remains a meaningful buffer through strong FDI coverage and still-broad export performance. Copom validated that cyclical transition with a fourth consecutive 25bp cut to 14.00%, but the central-bank tape makes the level-versus-direction distinction explicit: policy is easing at the margin while remaining deliberately restrictive in level.

The binding constraint has migrated away from contemporaneous activity/headline inflation toward credibility. Galípolo's 24 Jul framing—restrictive for longer and a higher dose than peers—pre-positioned the 5 Aug cut-plus-harder-statement package. Copom acknowledged disinflation and gradual activity moderation but refused to pre-commit, with still-hot labour conditions and rising 2027/2028 Focus expectations keeping de-anchoring at the center of the brake. Fiscal/debt arithmetic reinforces the same asymmetry. Regime: broadening disinflation + activity rollover + resilient external financing; action dovish / communication hawkish; credibility, not current growth, constrains the terminal depth of easing.

B. BRL MACRO SIGNAL SCOREBOARD

Growth: -
Supporting evidence:
Industrial output fell 1.8% m/m in June versus -0.8% expected, with all four broad categories down; July manufacturing PMI fell to 47.5 and services PMI to 49.7, taking the composite to 48.8. Orders, output and employment weakened across the survey tape, while Copom itself shifted to acknowledging gradual activity moderation. The evidence points to a material loss of momentum rather than a one-sector slowdown.

Labor: ~
Supporting evidence:
PMI employment weakened—manufacturing hiring ended and services employment fell for a second month—but the supplied archive contains no hard national labour release. Copom still described labour conditions as comparatively hot/resilient, leaving a genuine tension between survey hiring deterioration and the broader labour-market constraint.

Inflation: +
Supporting evidence:
July IPCA-15 undershot across headline and annual rates, diffusion fell from 60% to 48%, core momentum softened and underlying services improved. IGP-M, IPC-Fipe and IGP-DI corroborated the disinflation direction, while PMI input/output price inflation also eased. Copom explicitly acknowledged headline/core deceleration; labour-intensive services, administered electricity and supply-shock risk remain the sticky tail.

Financial Conditions: ~
Supporting evidence:
Copom cut the Selic to 14.00%, extending calibrated front-end easing while preserving a highly restrictive level and exceptional carry. But gross debt at 81.9% of GDP, R$110.7bn of June interest costs, de-anchored expectations and fiscal/election risk keep long-end term premium and BRL sensitivity elevated.

Policy Constraint: ~
Supporting evidence:
The cyclical bar to further easing has fallen materially, but the central-bank tape confirms that the structural bar remains high. Galípolo's “restrictive for longer / higher dose” framing and the harder 5 Aug statement show that Copom is willing to keep cutting only while realized disinflation and activity moderation outrun deterioration in longer-horizon expectations. The next decision is therefore genuinely conditional rather than mechanically guided.

Professional Market Commentary

The external-sector release is a clean positive on both the current-flow and financing legs. The current-account deficit narrowed to $2.33bn, while FDI reached $9.075bn, materially above consensus and comfortably exceeding the monthly current-account shortfall. The BCB reported that the 12-month current-account deficit narrowed to around 2.46% of GDP.

Key tape focus:

goods surplus widened to $8.8bn;

exports reached a record $36.4bn, up 24.8% y/y;

imports increased 15.3% to $27.6bn;

the services account remained in sizeable deficit;

the primary-income deficit remained around $6.5bn;

FDI inflows again materially exceeded the current-account financing requirement.

Comment — Economist Layer

The important signal is not simply the smaller monthly deficit. It is the combination of a stronger trade contribution and unusually strong direct-investment financing.

Signal hierarchy:

FDI overshoot;

narrowing current-account deficit;

record export performance;

continued structural services and income leakage.

The 12-month current-account deficit has continued to improve relative to earlier 2026 and full-year 2025 levels, while direct investment remains more than sufficient to finance it. On the June monthly arithmetic alone, FDI was close to four times the current-account deficit.

That materially improves the quality of Brazil's external financing. The country is not relying on short-duration portfolio flows to cover the external gap.

The composition is nevertheless not uniformly strong. Services remain structurally negative and the primary-income deficit remains large. Brazil's improved external position therefore continues to rely materially on the goods-trade engine.

The unresolved issue is how much of the record export performance represents durable volume improvement versus unusually favourable commodity/energy pricing.

State transition: improvement — the external-financing position strengthens further as the current-account gap narrows and FDI coverage rises materially.

Context from prior regional live file:
May had already delivered a smaller-than-expected current-account deficit alongside strong FDI, establishing a cleaner external-financing configuration. June extends that trajectory through a smaller deficit, record exports and an even larger FDI inflow. Brazil's external vulnerability therefore continues to recede rather than merely stabilize.

HF Take — Strategist Layer

This is modestly BRL-supportive but secondary to Copom and fiscal risk.

Reaction-function map:

stronger FDI coverage reduces balance-of-payments vulnerability;

the improving current-account ratio lowers the risk that monetary easing must be constrained primarily to defend external financing;

record exports provide an important cushion against weakening domestic activity;

persistent services and income deficits mean Brazil still benefits materially from a strong commodity/trade cycle;

a commodity-price reversal would weaken some of the apparent improvement.

For BRL, the cleanest implication is that the external account is not the binding constraint on gradual easing. The bigger domestic constraints remain inflation credibility and fiscal risk.

Brazil — IPCA-15 Mid-Month CPI MM / YY (Jul)

Release Date: 28 Jul 2026 | Local Time: 14:00
Importance: ****

Reuters Data

IPCA-15 Mid-Month CPI: 0,06% | Prior: 0,41% | Poll: 0,20% | Surprise: -0,140%

IPCA-15 Mid-Month CPI YY: 4,52% | Prior: 4,80% | Poll: 4,67% | Surprise: -0,150%

Professional Market Commentary

The institutional tape treated July IPCA-15 as a materially dovish inflation print rather than a simple headline undershoot. Inflation slowed to 0.06% m/m and 4.52% y/y, with the annual rate below the entire surveyed range.

Housing remained the main positive contribution through electricity tariffs, while food prices fell sharply.

Key tape focus:

stronger-than-expected disinflation;

softer goods and food;

negative fuel contribution;

administered electricity remained the main upward pressure;

diffusion fell sharply;

core measures softened;

DI futures rallied across the curve after the release;

institutional commentary broadly interpreted the print as strengthening the case for another Copom cut.

The important institutional emphasis was quality rather than only level. XP, BNP Paribas and other desks emphasized that the downside deviation was disseminated rather than concentrated in one volatile category.

The counterpoint was that labour-intensive services remained comparatively firm, preventing the print from becoming an unconditional inflation all-clear.

Comment — Economist Layer

This is the strongest Brazil consumer-disinflation print in the recent sequence because the downside surprise is both large and relatively broad.

Key signals:

0.06% m/m represents a sharp sequential deceleration from 0.41%;

4.52% y/y falls from 4.80% and moves essentially back to the 4.5% upper edge of the inflation tolerance range;

the print was below the entire surveyed range, making it more informative than an ordinary small consensus miss;

food and beverages fell 0.66%, offsetting the principal administered-price pressure from housing/electricity;

residential electricity rose 3.03% and supplied the month's largest individual positive impact;

fuels fell across the basket, including gasoline, ethanol and diesel, providing a reversible external-price disinflation channel;

more importantly, the domestic/free-price side also improved.

XP calculates that free prices accounted for around -0.15pp of its forecast error, with industrial goods, services and food generally softer than expected.

The directly readable XP note puts the average of core measures at 0.21% m/m versus 0.25% expected. The three-month SAAR of cores fell from 4.99% to 4.45%.

Diffusion dropped from 60% to 48%, confirming that the benign headline was not generated by one or two volatile items.

Underlying services also improved materially: XP's underlying-services 3m SAAR declined from 5.19% to 4.60%.

The residual concern is labour-intensive services. Their 3m SAAR eased only marginally from 7.30% to 7.11%, while the 12-month rate remained very elevated. This is the main reason not to extrapolate July mechanically into rapid convergence toward target.

The useful two-bucket interpretation is therefore fuel relief plus genuine domestic disinflation, rather than fuel relief alone.

But the domestic bucket is not uniformly benign: labour-sensitive services remain the sticky tail.

The level/momentum distinction is also important. Momentum has clearly improved, but 4.52% y/y is still around the upper edge of the tolerance range and private 2026 forecasts remained materially above the 3% target.

State transition: stabilization → broadening disinflation.

Context from prior regional live file:
June IPCA-15 had already delivered softer monthly/core detail, but annual inflation still accelerated to 4.80% and left Brazil in a conditionally easing-compatible rather than clean disinflation regime. July is the first clear extension of that improvement across headline, cores and diffusion while reversing the annual acceleration. The inflation constraint is therefore loosening materially, although sticky labour-intensive services prevent a full regime clearance.

HF Take — Strategist Layer

This is a meaningful dovish reaction-function event for Brazil.

Reaction-function map:

another 25bp Selic cut becomes easier to justify because both headline and underlying inflation momentum improved;

the key distinction versus earlier soft headline prints is the collapse in diffusion and improvement in cores, reducing the risk that Copom dismisses the result as volatile food/fuel noise;

DI futures' curve-wide rally immediately after the release is consistent with the market treating the print as policy-relevant rather than merely statistical;

nevertheless, persistent labour-intensive services and still-elevated inflation expectations argue against extrapolating July into an accelerated easing cycle;

administered electricity remains an upside channel, but its concentration makes the underlying signal cleaner than the headline alone implies;

for BRL, the immediate rates effect is ambiguous: lower inflation improves domestic macro quality but greater easing room reduces some carry support.

The correct interpretation is cut-enabling, not easing-unconstrained.

Next verification test: the 5 August Copom decision and statement.

order-book deterioration;

continued output contraction;

external-demand weakness;

moderating but still-elevated cost pressure.

Brazilian manufacturing therefore moves from fragile headline stabilization back to an overt restrictive-demand slowdown.

Brazil — Industrial Output MM / YY (Jun)

Release Date: 4 Aug 2026 | Local Time: 14:00
Importance: ****

Reuters Data

Industrial Output MM: -1,8% | Prior: -0,9% | Poll: -0,8% | Surprise: -1,00%

Industrial Output YY: 1,7% | Prior: 0,2% | Poll: 3,0% | Surprise: -1,30%

Professional Market Commentary

The professional tape treated June industrial production as clear evidence that Brazil's activity slowdown is becoming visible in hard data.

Output fell 1.8% m/m, the second consecutive decline and the largest monthly drop since December 2025, while the 1.7% y/y increase materially undershot the 3.0% consensus forecast.

All four broad economic categories weakened.

Key tape focus:

weakness was broad rather than confined to one industrial segment;

consumer goods led the monthly deterioration;

petroleum derivatives/biofuels and food contained identifiable sector-specific or weather-related drags;

the cumulative May-June decline partially unwound the strong January-April industrial advance;

industrial activity remained positive on a y/y and YTD basis, preventing the report from becoming an outright recession signal;

Pantheon Macroeconomics characterized industry as under pressure and argued that the figures reinforced the case for gradual monetary easing.

Comment — Economist Layer

This is the hard-data confirmation that the Brazil growth slowdown has moved beyond survey weakness, but the 1.8% headline contraction should not be interpreted as pure monetary-demand destruction.

Signal hierarchy:

broad sequential contraction;

two-month loss of industrial momentum;

weakness in consumer and capital-goods categories;

large downside miss in both Reuters headline measures;

technical/weather and petroleum-complex effects that amplify the monthly fall;

still-positive annual and first-half growth as the principal counter-signal.

Breadth:

all four broad economic categories declined;

16 of 25 industrial branches contracted;

semi- and non-durable consumer goods fell 4.1%;

durable goods fell 3.2%;

capital goods fell 1.1%;

intermediate goods fell 1.1%.

That breadth makes it difficult to dismiss June as one-industry noise.

Sector decomposition:

The largest individual drag was coke, petroleum products and biofuels at -5.9%, taking the sector's cumulative two-month decline to 11.8%.

Chemicals, food, pharmaceuticals, apparel, electronics/optics, transport equipment and rubber/plastics also contracted.

There is nevertheless a genuine technical overlay. IBGE attributed part of the food-industry weakness to rain disrupting sugarcane processing and hence sugar and ethanol production.

The petroleum-complex decline is also sufficiently concentrated that it should not be mapped one-for-one into aggregate final-demand weakness.

But the breadth prevents those effects from explaining the whole report.

Momentum versus level:

Industrial production remains up 1.7% y/y, 1.5% YTD and 0.7% over twelve months.

The regime is therefore loss of momentum from an earlier expansion, not yet an absolute industrial collapse.

The sequence is nevertheless sharp. The first four months of 2026 had generated a cumulative 4.4% increase; May and June subsequently removed 2.7% of that gain.

The investment composition is especially policy-relevant. Capital-goods production was down 5.4% over H1, with mixed-use and agricultural capital goods particularly weak.

Unlike rain-sensitive food production or petroleum volatility, persistent weakness in capital goods is more naturally consistent with transmission from exceptionally restrictive real interest rates into investment demand.

This also materially strengthens the PMI cross-read. Hard output was already weakening before the July manufacturing survey reported a further deterioration in demand.

State transition: deterioration — the early-2026 industrial expansion has rolled over into a broad two-month contraction, although technical sector effects and still-positive annual growth argue against calling this an industrial collapse.

Context from prior regional live file:
Industrial production had improved sequentially through the opening months of 2026, with April extending the cumulative January-April gain to 4.4%. May-June then erased 2.7% of that advance, while June's weakness broadened across all major categories. The industrial trajectory therefore shifts from constrained expansion to a clearer activity slowdown, with investment weakness becoming increasingly important.

HF Take — Strategist Layer

This is a materially dovish growth input for Copom because it confirms that restrictive monetary conditions are increasingly visible in realized activity rather than only sentiment surveys.

Reaction-function map:

the double miss strengthens the evidence that economic momentum is slowing;

broad category weakness gives the report greater policy weight than a petroleum-only production shock would have;

capital-goods weakness is particularly relevant because it provides a cleaner investment-side transmission channel from the very high Selic;

the simultaneous deterioration in consumer-goods production indicates that weakness is not confined to corporate capex;

the soft July IPCA-15 and easing PMI price indices now sit alongside weaker hard activity, producing a more coherent disinflation + slowdown configuration;

nevertheless, positive annual/YTD output, identifiable weather effects and petroleum-sector volatility prevent the report from justifying an accelerated easing cycle by itself;

persistent inflation expectations and a still-resilient labour market remain the principal constraints on converting weaker industrial activity into larger cuts.

The distinction between whether to ease and how fast to ease is therefore central.

The industrial report materially supports another calibrated cut; it is much weaker evidence for moving beyond the existing 25bp pace.

For the curve, the cleanest implication is supportive for front-end easing expectations. The long end remains exposed to fiscal and inflation-risk premia that weaker industrial production does not resolve.

For BRL, the signal is two-sided: slower activity and additional easing room reduce the domestic carry-growth advantage at the margin, while an orderly slowdown that helps restore inflation convergence is preferable to a stagflationary configuration.
