DATA WINDOW: 2 August 2026 to 5 September 2026

A. CAD WEEK — MACRO SYNTHESIS

Canada moved from recession-risk misclassification toward demonstrated H1 resilience, but the stronger starting point did not translate into a clean Q3 re-acceleration. Q2 GDP expanded 3.3% annualized, the Q1 contraction was revised away, consumption and investment strengthened, manufacturing remained expansionary and the external accounts temporarily generated an exceptional cushion. The handoff then weakened: July GDP was approximately flat, the trade windfall normalized sharply and August employment reversed while wage growth fell to 2.0%. The resulting growth regime is best described as **strong Q2 recovery / weaker forward handoff without evidence of outright contraction**.

Inflation simultaneously moved from clean June disinflation toward a two-sided persistence watch. July headline CPI reached 3.0% and energy/refining pressures increased, but preferred core measures remained around 2%, ex-energy producer inflation stayed soft and wage growth decelerated materially. The 2 September BoC communication hardened the prior hawkish drift into an **emerging hiking bias**: Macklem explicitly prioritized inflation risk, characterized 3.0% inflation as too high for comfort and, in wire-reported Q&A, opened the possibility of multiple hikes. Strong Q2 growth and July employment had removed the need for growth protection, allowing the Bank to lean against persistent energy risk despite the worsening trade backdrop. The September 4 labour reversal then immediately challenged that stance by weakening both the demand and wage-persistence channels. The final policy state is therefore **hawkish hold / emerging hiking bias in communication, but with a materially higher evidentiary hurdle for action after the August labour data**.

B. CAD MACRO SIGNAL SCOREBOARD

Growth: ~

Supporting evidence:
Q2 GDP rebounded 3.3% annualized, Q1 contraction was revised away, consumption, business investment and residential investment strengthened, and manufacturing remained expansionary. However, July GDP was approximately flat, housing starts weakened, the July trade surplus normalized sharply and August small-business/labour signals deteriorated. Strong realized growth therefore coexists with a materially softer forward handoff.

Labor: -

Supporting evidence:
July employment rose 75.1k and unemployment fell to 6.4%, initially confirming a labour recovery. August reversed that improvement with employment -41.7k, weaker participation and employment rate, while broad wage growth slowed to 2.0%. Strong hours worked and manufacturing hiring prevent a collapse interpretation, but the state transition at window close is clearly toward softer labour conditions.

Inflation: ~

Supporting evidence:
July headline CPI rose to 3.0% and short-run core momentum firmed, while energy/refining pressure remained elevated. Against that, median and trim stayed around 2%, ex-gasoline CPI was 2.2%, shelter disinflation continued, ex-energy producer prices fell and wage growth slowed sharply. Direct energy pressure is established; broad second-round persistence is not.

Financial Conditions: +

Supporting evidence:
The Q2 current account swung into a large surplus, real export volumes were strong and foreign purchases of Canadian government bonds reached record levels. The external funding and balance-of-payments cushion improved materially even though July subsequently removed much of the exceptional energy/metals trade windfall.

Policy Constraint: -

Supporting evidence:
The September 2 BoC decision materially tightened the reaction-function constraint despite the unchanged 2.25% rate. Macklem emphasized sharper inflation risks, described 3.0% inflation as too high for comfort and signaled that multiple hikes were possible, while strong growth and labour data had removed the case for policy insurance against recession. The stance therefore shifted from a symmetric hold toward a hawkish hold with an emerging hiking bias. The September 4 employment decline and 2.0% wage growth subsequently raised the hurdle for implementation, but did not undo the communication shift.

TUESDAY 4 AUG 2026

CANADA — INTERNATIONAL MERCHANDISE TRADE (JUN)

Release Date: 4 Aug 2026 | Local Time: 14:30
Importance: ***

1. Reuters Data

Trade Balance C$: 4,20B | Prior: 3,67B | Poll: 3,00B | Surprise: 1,200B

2. Professional Market Commentary

Institutional commentary treated the fourth consecutive trade surplus as constructive, but consistently downweighted the nominal headline.

Statistics Canada's original release showed a roughly C$3.9B surplus, with exports reaching a record C$77.5B and imports also rising. The agency itself highlighted a major FX distortion: CAD depreciation materially lifted Canadian-dollar trade values, while both exports and imports fell when measured in US dollars.

RBC emphasized the much stronger real signal: Q2 export volumes rose around 23% annualized and 9.5% y/y, while equipment imports pointed to firmer Canadian business investment. Gold and energy remained unusually volatile contributors, while underlying flows excluding those categories were more stable.

Desjardins also stressed strong real export volumes and increasing non-US diversification, although its initial estimate that net exports would drag on Q2 GDP was subsequently falsified by the national accounts.

3. Comment — Economist Layer

The headline surplus is less informative than its composition.

Key signals:

* nominal exports reached record levels, partly reflecting CAD depreciation
* Q2 export volumes accelerated sharply
* gold exports were an important positive swing factor
* energy export values declined as oil prices retraced
* underlying flows excluding gold and energy appeared more stable
* processing-unit and electronic-equipment imports pointed to a developing data-centre / capex channel
* non-US destinations continued to gain share.

The import composition is particularly constructive. Higher equipment imports mechanically subtract from net exports but can simultaneously represent stronger productive investment.

The release therefore combined genuine external-volume strength with substantial nominal distortion from FX and volatile commodities.

State transition: continuation.

Context from prior regional live file:

May had already produced a large trade surplus with record export values, but real export volumes had been much less impressive. June improves the quality of the external signal through exceptionally strong Q2 export volumes, although gold, energy and FX translation continue to distort the monthly balance. The external recovery therefore looks more durable than May alone suggested without yet establishing a broad Canadian growth regime.

4. HF Take — Strategist Layer

The release reduced growth-side pressure on the BoC at the margin but did not create a tightening signal.

The useful policy information was:

* external demand was recovering
* export volumes were genuinely stronger
* investment-related imports were constructive
* the economy appeared better able to absorb restrictive policy.

Against that:

* the nominal surplus overstated the clean real signal
* commodity and currency effects were unusually large
* looming trade-policy escalation threatened the durability of the improvement.

The strategic read was less growth-driven easing pressure, not renewed tightening pressure.

CANADA — S&P GLOBAL MANUFACTURING PMI (JUL)

Release Date: 4 Aug 2026 | Local Time: 15:30
Importance: ***

1. Reuters Data

S&P Global Mfg PMI SA: 53,5 | Prior: 53,0

2. Professional Market Commentary

The tape showed continued manufacturing expansion with an increasingly uncomfortable growth/inflation mix.

The PMI rose to its highest level since June 2022, with output, domestic new orders and employment improving.

But export orders declined for a second consecutive month. Input-cost inflation reached a four-year high amid supply shortages and delivery delays, and manufacturers raised selling prices.

Business confidence simultaneously fell, leaving a configuration of stronger current activity, weak external demand and greater cost pressure.

3. Comment — Economist Layer

The 53.5 headline should not be interpreted mechanically as an equivalent acceleration in final demand.

Key signals:

* output and domestic new orders improved
* hiring strengthened
* export orders weakened
* input inflation reached a four-year high
* selling-price pass-through increased
* confidence weakened
* finished-goods inventories rose partly because of shipping disruption.

The inverted supplier-delivery component matters. Longer delivery times can raise the PMI even when they reflect scarcity rather than demand.

The growth signal was nevertheless real because output, orders and employment all strengthened.

The more important BoC signal was therefore not 53.5 itself but the combination of acute input-cost inflation and higher selling prices.

State transition: continuation.

Context from prior regional live file:

Canadian manufacturing had already shifted from stagnation toward expansion in spring, with output, orders and hiring improving while geopolitical disruption raised costs. July extends the activity upswing but makes the expansion increasingly supply-constrained. Manufacturing therefore remains stronger in activity but less benign for inflation than the broader Canadian disinflation story.

4. HF Take — Strategist Layer

The release reduced the BoC's tolerance for additional inflation deterioration without independently creating a tightening case.

The reaction-function test became whether manufacturing cost pressure would migrate into CPI and wages.

The strategic configuration was:

domestic manufacturing resilient + external demand softer + cost-push pressure intensifying.

That reinforced a hold with greater hawkish-tail sensitivity.

May had generated an exceptional employment rebound after April weakness, but June remained insufficient to prove a durable turn. The strategic read was materially less dovish, with tightening still dependent on inflation persistence rather than labour strength alone.

This creates an important distinction between near-term construction output and new supply formation.

The current pipeline can support residential investment for a while even as fewer new projects begin.

For inflation, the horizon matters:

near term — softer shelter CPI remains dovish;
medium term — persistently inadequate starts could eventually constrain rental supply and reintroduce shelter pressure.

State transition: deterioration.

Context from prior regional live file:

June had already pushed starts sharply below consensus and weakened the six-month trend. July extends that weakness with another large miss, although the near-flat trend prevents classification as a construction collapse. Housing therefore remains a persistent supply-side weak pocket within an otherwise improving activity mix.

4. HF Take — Strategist Layer

This was a modestly dovish growth input for the BoC but not an immediate policy trigger.

The release limited the breadth of the Canadian recovery while the completion pipeline and stable resale demand prevented a severe housing-downturn interpretation.

persistent growth-side soft spot now + potential medium-term supply constraint later.

Release Date: 18 Mar 2026 | Local Time: 14:45 ET

Reuters Data

BoC Rate Decision: 2.25% | Prior: 2.25% | Poll: 2.25% | Surprise: 0.000%

Professional Market Commentary

The hold was fully expected, but the statement and press conference were richer than the action. The BoC held at 2.25%, said the economy remains in excess supply, flagged the labour market as soft, and noted that CPI excluding taxes and core measures are all now close to 2%. At the same time, it explicitly described the war in Iran as a new layer of uncertainty and said it would look through the immediate inflation impact, while not allowing high energy prices to broaden into persistent inflation.

Comment — Economist Layer

This is a genuine policy fork, not a simple “hold and done” message. The domestic baseline is soft: growth has weakened, unemployment has risen, and core inflation has come down materially. But the oil shock has created an upside inflation risk just as growth is weakening. That is textbook stagflation-style policy tension. The important point is that the BoC did not validate a renewed hiking bias; it kept the burden of proof on persistence in inflation spillovers, not on the first-round energy move.

State transition: no material change in action, regime fork in forward path.

Context from prior regional live file:

The prior DM live file had January’s BoC hold framed as a tactical pause inside an easing-biased regime. Since then, the live sequence has added soft growth, weak labour, softer housing starts, and a cleaner February CPI baseline. March’s hold therefore continues the pause, but with more two-sided uncertainty because the oil shock now interrupts what had become a progressively more dovish domestic data path.

HF Take — Strategist Layer

The BoC is still much closer to a future cut debate than to a hike debate, but timing has become less clean. Near term, the Bank has room to wait because core inflation is close to target and the economy has slack. The decisive verification test is March CPI: if oil lifts headline but core spillovers stay contained, the cut path reopens later in 2026.
