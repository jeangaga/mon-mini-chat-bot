MULTI-RELEASE CONSOLIDATION RULE

Consolidate into a single block only when releases are part of the same official publication from the same statistical agency at the same timestamp.

Examples of valid consolidation:
- CPI and Core CPI (same BLS release)
- PCE and Core PCE (same BEA release)
- GDP headline and GDP components (same ONS / BEA release)
- Trade balance, exports, and imports (same Census / StatCan release)
- Employment change and unemployment rate (same StatCan / BLS release)

Do not consolidate releases that are:
- from different agencies
- from different official sources
- released at different times on the same day
- thematically related but institutionally separate

Examples of invalid consolidation:
- CPI (BLS) and ISM Manufacturing (ISM) on the same day → two separate blocks
- GDP (ONS) and Industrial Production (ONS, different release) → two separate blocks
- Trade Balance and Employment Change arriving the same morning → two separate blocks
- Turkey Current Account (CBRT) and Turkey Industrial Production (TurkStat) on the same day → two separate blocks

Rule of thumb:
If the data came from one press release, one block.
If it came from two press releases, two blocks, regardless of how close in time they were published.

Operational test:
If the indicators are published in the same official release note / bulletin / press release document, consolidate them.
If they require opening two separate official release pages or bulletins, keep them separate.

Scoring logic:
Combine:
1. intrinsic importance of the release
2. size / quality of the surprise
3. usefulness for deeper interpretation, JGM follow-up, or regime mapping

Interpretation:
- * = low importance
  Minor release, low surprise, limited policy or regime value, low priority for deeper follow-up
- ** = secondary importance
  Useful domestic signal, but not a major policy hinge or regime-defining release
- *** = important
  Clear macro signal, meaningful surprise and/or relevant policy implications, worth close reading
- **** = highest importance
  Major release and/or major surprise and/or decisive regime / policy / market relevance; priority for deeper JGM-style follow-up

Application rule:
- importance is assessed relative to the selected economy’s macro story, not by a fixed global scale
- a release may rank higher if it resolves the main contradiction in that country's live file even if the calendar importance is only medium
- do not overuse ****
- only a limited number of releases in a currency-specific file should receive **** unless the selected currency’s week is exceptionally dense




THREE-LAYER PM READING HEADER — MANDATORY (NEW v3.6)

Purpose:
For a high-volume workflow spanning many independent single-CCY files, the first screen must deliver a high-value quick read without requiring the reader to scroll through Sections 1–4. The detailed research remains intact for selective drill-down and later historical reference.

Position and exact plain-text field names (immediately under Importance, before Section 1):
PM FLASH:
PM READ-THROUGH:
PM DEBATE & KEY TAKEAWAYS:

Inside the third field, prefer the compact sublabels 'Debate:' (when actual disagreement exists) and 'Key takeaways:'. If differences reflect complementary emphases rather than genuine opposition, frame them as a tension or contrasting interpretations without inventing a dispute. If no meaningful professional disagreement is evidenced, use 'Professional insight:' if sourced, followed by 'Key takeaways:'. If no meaningful human insight exists, give the grounded key takeaway alone; never fabricate a human voice or fill the line with generic boilerplate.

Division of labor:
- PM FLASH = WHAT PRINTED? Give the relevant actual versus poll and prior (when present), surprise and one or two decisive component drivers. Facts, not a market call. Do not recompute or silently correct a mechanically locked Reuters surprise; explain an identified feed anomaly in detailed analysis.
- PM READ-THROUGH = WHAT DID THIS PRINT ADD? State its incremental confirmation, qualification, contradiction or small change to the accumulated macro assessment. Do not pretend one release proves an economy-wide recovery, inflation regime change or cycle turn. A print may leave the assessment unchanged.
- PM DEBATE & KEY TAKEAWAYS = WHAT DOES PROFESSIONAL RESEARCH ADD? Promote the most differentiated verified human-scout interpretation, the important debate / tension, and one or two non-obvious mechanisms, contradictions or next tests. Draw selectively from Sections 2, 3 and 4; do not merely repeat Flash or Read-through.

Attribution separation — STRICT:
- Do NOT include any analyst names, economist names, bank names or institution names in ANY of the three PM summary fields, including when summarizing human-source disagreement. Explain the actual competing interpretations instead.
- Section 2 MUST retain the names, institutions and provenance of material verified human voices. Preserve their distinct positions rather than merging them into anonymous generic consensus.
- The PM summary MUST represent those views faithfully and only when documented. Do not fabricate opposition, implied quotes or confidence.
- Institutional primary data may still be identified by indicator / country in the release heading and detailed sections.

Length and style:
- Approximately 75–110 words combined for a typical important publication; shorter for * / **; allow more for a genuinely information-rich **** release only when justified.
- Aim for a useful roughly 20–30-second skim, not a mechanical word-count target.
- Write in dense, coherent sentences and avoid stacked micro-paragraphs, repetitive headline paraphrases and generic labels.
- The third field may be slightly longer than each of the first two, because the professional debate and key research insights are its main value-add.
- Prefer one sharp tension over listing four or five minor points. Name important numbers when they are central to the interpretation.
- Exclude gratuitous ECB / central-bank, front-end rates, FX, equity or trade conclusions. Include policy and markets only when this specific release meaningfully changes the relevant reaction function / scenario or when attributable release-window pricing has been established.
- The summary must reflect uncertainty and evidence gaps, not promote a hypothesis to an established fact.
- Build the full evidence analysis first and synthesize the PM header from it. If later validated facts materially modify the release, patch the three PM fields together with the affected underlying section. Otherwise preserve them unchanged.

Illustrative summary only (all named source attribution belongs in the full Section 2):
GERMANY — PRODUCTION IN INDUSTRY / INDUSTRIAL OUTPUT (AUG)

A. Economist Assessment — start with one or two dense paragraphs identifying the primary economic signal, its quality and the key caveat. Explain what changed incrementally; do not restate every PM summary line.
B. Detailed Evidence & Economic Mechanisms — organize the substantive evidence into a small number of thematic analyses, with compact tables where figures clarify the mechanism.
C. State Transition & Historical Context — conclude with the EXACT mandatory 'State transition:' field, followed by the standalone EXACT 'Context from prior regional live file:' field according to the rules below.

The A/B/C subheadings are recommended for *** / **** and analytically complex releases, but unnecessary subdivisions can be omitted for * / **. '3. Comment — Economist Layer', 'State transition:' and the context field remain fixed regardless of subheading choices.

Evidence-preserving compression — STRICT:
- Compress repetitive interpretation and formatting, NOT material underlying research.
- Preserve decision-relevant sector/component decomposition, prior revisions, level versus rate-of-change distinctions, seasonal and base effects, three-month / quarter-level arithmetic, population / survey composition, margin / price-pass-through channels and measurement caveats where applicable.
- Preserve non-obvious economic mechanisms: orders versus backlog conversion, output versus demand, inventory timing, construction versus manufacturing, price versus volume, data revisions and distortions.

- Integrate the substantive causal reasoning from validated human sources and JGM where it improves interpretation; keep original human attribution in Section 2 rather than artificially repeating every name here.

SECTION 4 — HF TAKE — STRATEGIST LAYER

This is the decision-oriented macro PM assessment. It must be valuable even when the publication has no material immediate central-bank, rates, FX or equity implication.

Build it from verified release evidence, differentiated validated scout/human interpretations, the broader current and historical release sequence, and JGM constraints where provided.

Default structure — use tight paragraphs rather than automatic bullet lists:
1. What changed: Identify the principal incremental information for the existing macro thesis and which earlier release it confirms, offsets or qualifies.
2. Why it matters: Explain the most important causal mechanism or cross-release tension, including a valuable differentiated professional hypothesis where supported; state the limitations.
3. What to verify next: Specify the 2–5 most discriminating follow-up series, data components, revisions or conditions that would strengthen or weaken this interpretation.
4. Macro configuration: Close with one compact cumulative macro configuration; keep it conditional and proportional to the release's real significance.

Rules:
- Do not copy the PM Flash or re-list the Reuters numbers without analytical purpose.
- Avoid inflated inferences: one print normally changes the confidence in a hypothesis or quarterly arithmetic, not an entire economic regime.
- Do not force ECB / Fed / BoE / BoJ / central-bank, FX, front-end rate or trade calls on ordinary releases. Include those only when material, properly supported and directly connected to the release or a clearly specified scenario.
- Keep genuinely material policy-timing, rates, FX and cross-asset implications when evidence warrants them; the rule prevents boilerplate, not useful trading analysis.
- Conditional language and explicit distinctions between hard evidence and hypotheses are mandatory; no invented market repricing or deterministic triggers.
- One short JGM constraint insight may be integrated when explicitly supplied.
- For consolidated multi-indicator publications, write one HF Take that prioritizes the decisive component and reconciles important internal contradictions.
- Prefer roughly one or two substantive paragraphs plus a verification line and Macro configuration for *** / ****; much shorter where evidence is thin. Do not sacrifice distinctive content to an arbitrary length target.

## DATA WINDOW CALENDAR-WEEK RULE

The `DATA WINDOW` must use a **Sunday-to-Saturday calendar-week convention**, not the exact first and last release dates.

Rules:

1. **Start date = Sunday opening the first included calendar week.**

   * If the first relevant release occurs on Monday, use the immediately preceding Sunday.
   * If the first relevant release occurs on Sunday, use that Sunday.
   * More generally, always roll the first included release date back to the Sunday beginning that week.

2. **End date = Saturday closing the last included calendar week.**

   * If the last relevant release occurs Monday-Friday, extend the DATA WINDOW through the following Saturday.
   * If the last relevant release occurs on Saturday, use that Saturday.
   * If releases span several weeks, use the Saturday ending the final included week.

3. The purpose is to capture the complete monitoring week, including occasional releases occurring on Sunday, even though most macro releases occur Monday-Friday.

4. Do **not** define the DATA WINDOW as:

   * first release date → last release date;
   * Monday → Friday;
   * rolling business-day boundaries.

5. This calendar rule applies to both:

   * live weekly macro files;
   * end-of-week finalized macro notes.

Example:

First included release: Monday 20 July 2026
Last included release: Friday 7 August 2026

Correct header:

`DATA WINDOW: 19 July 2026 to 8 August 2026`

because Sunday 19 July opens the first included week and Saturday 8 August closes the final included week.
